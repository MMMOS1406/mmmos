Three ordinary choices quietly shape your net worth, and none of them feels dramatic. Start with time. Say you invest $300 every month, assuming an average 8 percent annual return. If you start today, that portfolio grows to about $447,108 after 30 years. But if you wait ten years before you start, contributing the same $300 a month for the remaining 20 years, you end up with only about $176,706. That ten year delay costs you $270,402. Next, think about debt. Say you borrow $30,000 for 5 years. At a 5% rate, you will pay back about $33,968 in total. At a 9% rate, you will pay back about $37,365 in total. That gap in the rate costs you an extra $3,397. Finally, look at fees. Imagine you invest $20,000 and leave it alone for 25 years, earning about 7 percent a year before costs. With a fund charging 0.1 percent a year, you would end up with about $106,041. With a fund charging 1 percent a year, you would end up with about $85,837. That fee difference costs you $20,204. Start early, borrow carefully, and check every fee before you commit.
