Small habits and quiet rates decide where your money ends up. Start with time. Say you invest $150 every month, assuming an average 7 percent annual return. If you start today, that portfolio grows to about $182,996 after 30 years. But if you wait six years before you start, contributing the same $150 a month for the remaining 24 years, you end up with only about $111,585. That six year delay costs you $71,411. Next, look at your home loan. Say you borrow $180,000 for 15 years. At a 5.5% rate, you will pay back about $264,735 in total. At a 6.5% rate, you will pay back about $282,239 in total. That one point of interest costs you an extra $17,504. Finally, look at fund fees. Imagine you invest $40,000 and leave it alone for 25 years, earning about 7 percent a year before costs. With a fund charging 0.1 percent a year, you would end up with about $212,081. With a fund charging 0.9 percent a year, you would end up with about $175,770. That fee difference costs you $36,311. Start early, shop your rate, and watch every fee.
