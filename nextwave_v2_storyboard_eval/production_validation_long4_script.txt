Three decisions decide how much of your money you actually keep. Start with when you begin. Say you invest $200 every month, assuming an average 7 percent annual return. If you start today, that portfolio grows to about $243,994 after 30 years. But if you wait seven years before you start, contributing the same $200 a month for the remaining 23 years, you end up with only about $136,439. That seven year delay costs you $107,555. Next, look at your home loan. Say you borrow $200,000 for 30 years. At a 4% rate, you will pay back about $343,739 in total. At a 5% rate, you will pay back about $386,512 in total. That one point of interest costs you an extra $42,773. Finally, check the fees on your investments. Imagine you invest $15,000 and leave it alone for 30 years, earning about 7 percent a year before costs. With a fund charging 0.2 percent a year, you would end up with about $107,952. With a fund charging 1 percent a year, you would end up with about $86,152. That fee difference costs you $21,800. Start early, shop your rate, and question every fee.
