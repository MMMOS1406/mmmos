Three financial choices decide whether your money works for you or against you. Start with time. Say you invest $500 every month, assuming an average 6 percent annual return. If you start today, that portfolio grows to about $346,497 after 25 years. But if you wait eight years before you start, contributing the same $500 a month for the remaining 17 years, you end up with only about $176,616. That eight year delay costs you $169,881. Next, think about your mortgage. Say you borrow $350,000 for 30 years. At a 6% rate, you will pay back about $755,434 in total. At a 7% rate, you will pay back about $838,281 in total. That one point of interest costs you an extra $82,847. Finally, look at fund fees. Imagine you invest $30,000 and leave it alone for 20 years, earning about 7 percent a year before costs. With a fund charging 0.15 percent a year, you would end up with about $112,879. With a fund charging 1 percent a year, you would end up with about $96,214. That fee difference costs you $16,665. Start early, negotiate your rate, and never ignore fees.
